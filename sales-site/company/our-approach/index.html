From a vehicle listing to a showroom visit

Follow one vehicle from the day it enters inventory through the moment a shopper asks to see it.

Book a walkthrough ↗View platform screens ↓

Inside the platform

Select a screenshot to inspect the controls at full size. Screens use demonstration inventory and shopper records.

Follow one car all the way to the salesperson

Start with a question a salesperson can answer only by looking at the car. Does the third row fold flat? Is the advertised cash down for this stock number? Can the shopper see it Saturday? Those questions reveal whether the website, voice assistant and private workspace agree.

Write down the dealership’s version of the truth

We begin with one vehicle, its VIN and stock number, the approved price and the pictures the store can publish. The used-car manager identifies what can be read from the VIN, what requires a sticker and what needs a walk out to the lot. That distinction guides what the assistant can tell a shopper.

Watch the handoff from both sides

A shopper opens that listing on a phone, asks a question and requests a test drive. A second person plays the salesperson and opens the private record. We compare the exact car, callback details, appointment receipt and any trade or seating note. If the salesperson has to ask the shopper to start over, the handoff needs more work.

Change something that matters

Move the appointment, sell the vehicle or change Saturday’s opening time. Recheck the places the original fact appeared, including the listing, booking flow and feed. That exercise makes it clear which system owns each change and which outside provider controls the timing.

Give the store a launch decision it can make

An independent lot might be ready once a single salesperson can publish a car and answer its lead. A franchise rooftop may also need manufacturer sign-off or CRM delivery evidence. We identify those dependencies, put a name against each and agree on what the dealer will verify before switching domains.

Questions dealers ask

What happens if our current website already works well?

Start with the problem that brought you here. A phone receptionist or standalone chat widget may be enough. During discovery we identify the existing provider, available inventory context and the person who will receive the handoff.

What if the shopper asks something the AI cannot verify?

The useful next step is a staff question linked to the car. The salesperson can inspect the vehicle or its documentation and respond with a specific answer.

How do you decide that a feature is ready for our store?

A named dealership employee should be able to repeat the shopper task and find its result. Outside requirements such as official manufacturer approval or an authorized DMS feed carry their own acceptance evidence.

Bring the question you answered twice last week

Bring one public vehicle listing and the words a shopper actually used. We’ll use that example to show what the shopper sees, what the assistant can verify and what the employee needs at the other end.
