# Dealer reputation management

The first pilot is a single rooftop. A shopper like Ben can call the dealer, book by phone, take a test drive, and buy a car. The sales team can open his client record and see the vehicle and signed amount. Reputation uses the attended visit or purchase to identify a real customer experience. A callback number alone gives the sales team no basis to enroll him in automated review outreach.

## Delivery plan

| Release | Dealer workflow | Acceptance criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Review link and customer list | In **Reputation**, a manager enters the review link copied from the dealership’s Google Business Profile and opens it to check the location. Staff can copy the same link and open a client’s history. The list shows each shopper’s most recent attended visit or signed sale. | A dealer can run this without API credentials. A booking alone never qualifies. Correcting attendance removes a shopper whose only qualifying event was that visit. Repeated purchases and visits yield one customer row. Another rooftop cannot read the link or clients. Saving two conflicting edits returns a conflict. |
| 2. Permission-based invitations | Dealership owner enables a sender, selects channel and delay, previews a neutral invitation, and sends an internal test. Staff see queued, suppressed, sent, failed or delivery unknown in the client record. Channel opt-out and one-click campaign pause remain available. | Pilot with one approved sender, one internal recipient and one authorized dealership. Verify purpose-specific consent, STOP/unsubscribe, store-local sending windows, sender connection, available balance and recent-contact cooldown immediately before dispatch. A delivery timeout requires provider reconciliation before retry. Test with the sender disconnected and with insufficient credit. |
| 3. Google review inbox | Owner signs into Google, chooses a verified location and authorizes the connection. Staff read reviews, assign an owner and prepare a reply. An authorized manager previews exactly what will appear publicly and confirms publication. Recheck the published reply with Google. | Approved API access and the correct location are prerequisites. Page through all reviews. Deduplicate notifications, refresh changed or removed reviews, reconcile missed events, expire caches and show last successful sync. A revoked token stops the connector and prompts reconnection. Exercise two locations, identical reviewer names, simultaneous reply edits, provider timeout and disconnect. |
| 4. Reporting and additional channels | Show actual invitation delivery, first-party link clicks, outstanding staff tasks and response times. Offer a website reviews component only where display rights and attribution have been verified. Assess additional providers one at a time. | Report each metric with its source, denominator and date range. A review-link click is only a click. A shared name or click cannot prove a specific client wrote a review. Make each channel’s read, reply, display and solicitation capabilities explicit. |

**What ships first.** Release 1 uses dealership PostgreSQL. The public Google link points directly to the dealer-selected destination. Copying it sends no message, posts no reply and creates no wallet charge. Releases 2–4 are planned. [Google’s instructions for finding the review link](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en)

## Invitations, metering and the sales handoff

Use a neutral request such as “Thanks for visiting Southern Ohio Auto Sales. If you’d like to share your experience, you can leave a Google review here.” Offer it to eligible shoppers regardless of their feedback or whether they bought. Attendance corrections, cancels and opt-outs should update the queue before dispatch. Use the same eligibility criteria for every shopper and avoid incentives and star-rating prompts. [Google Maps contribution policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114) · [FTC review guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers)

Store the invitation’s rooftop, client ID, source event ID, channel-specific permission and time, template version, sender identity and provider receipt in tenant-scoped PostgreSQL. Separate review outreach consent from an earlier request for a text about a vehicle. Reserve the expected SMS charge against the dealer’s wallet, consume or release the reservation according to the billing policy and reconcile against the provider’s billable segments. Add a unique constraint on the outbound request ID and ledger charge ID. Test repeated callbacks and out-of-order delivery updates. An attended drive followed by a sale inside the cooldown gets one invitation. Confirm the existing Twilio/Resend connection and the SMS wallet debit path per pilot before offering automated sends.

## Google access and AI replies

Engineering owns an approved Google Cloud project. The dealer signs in and explicitly selects a verified location they manage. Bind that location to the matching rooftop, store encrypted credentials server-side, restrict staff by role, and support revocation and disconnection. Google currently asks API applicants to manage an active verified profile for at least 60 days. Confirm quota approval before scheduling the connected pilot. [API prerequisites](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/prereqs) · [Review API operations](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/review-data)

Use notifications and periodic reconciliation to refresh the authorized location. Limit stored provider content to the permitted temporary cache and apply its retention policy. Keep first-party operational metrics and reply approval receipts separate. Review provider terms before sending any review text to an external AI service. Drafts use a dealership-approved tone. Staff review and approve a specific reply before it is sent. Keep phone numbers, financing information, VINs, private notes and the client’s contract value out of public replies. [Google API policies](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/policies) · [Notification setup](https://developers.google.com/my-business/content/notification-setup)

## Pilot decision

The dealer owner supplies the correct Google review link for Release 1. The next dependency for invitation delivery is a dealer-approved sending identity and policy for review-specific consent. The separate dependency for a synchronized public-review inbox is Google API approval and the dealer’s authorization. Demonstrate the flow internally before enabling invitations for real shoppers. Judge the pilot by duplicate sends, suppressed sends, unresolved delivery attempts, reply turnaround and dealer time saved.
